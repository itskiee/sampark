# 3-minute demo video: script

Total 180 s, 370 spoken words (about 123 words per minute: calm pace). Generated by `report/video/make_video_kit.py`; captions in `captions.srt`.

## Recording checklist

- Screen 1920×1080, browser zoom 100 %, dashboard in a clean browser window (no signed-in tabs, no bookmarks bar).
- Never show `.env`, a terminal with tokens, or the Copernicus login page.
- Maps and screens only: **no images of victims** (brief, responsibility note).
- The 'EDUCATIONAL PROTOTYPE' label must be visible (dashboard header).
- Live run: record the real run and speed it up; show the caption 'sped up'.
- Voice: read the narration below; add `captions.srt` as captions (Clipchamp: Captions → upload, or the platform's subtitle upload).
- Before recording, check every number spoken against the dashboard of the case-study run.

## Script

| Time | Show | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:12 | Title card (title_card.png). | Sampark. Give it an area and a flood date, and it tells rescue teams where the flood hit, what was damaged, and who is cut off, from free satellite data only. |
| 0:12–0:30 | Dashboard: open the Trishuli case-study run; overview map. | This is the August 2026 debris flood in Nepal's Bhote Koshi and Trishuli valley. The system uses only what the brief allows: Sentinel-1 radar, Sentinel-2 when the sky is clear, the Copernicus elevation model, and OpenStreetMap as it was before the flood. |
| 0:30–0:52 | Sidebar 'New analysis': type a box and a date, click Run; show the live progress (speed the recording up and show the caption 'sped up'). | Judges can choose any area and date. Here I start a new analysis. Every step reports its status, and if something is missing, say clouds over the optical image, that step is skipped with a reason and the rest still runs. |
| 0:52–1:15 | Tab 'Where did the flood hit?': flood map with confidence; zoom into the valley. | Where did the flood hit? Radar images before and after are compared only on the same orbit track. Terrain masks remove slopes where water cannot stand, and every area gets a confidence level. Here, 15.6 square kilometres are potentially affected. |
| 1:15–1:40 | Tabs 'What was damaged' then 'Who is cut off': cut-off map, then one settlement sentence. | What was damaged? Over three thousand pre-event buildings, 80 kilometres of road and 163 bridges are potentially affected. Who is cut off? Comparing the road network before and after finds 215 settlements likely cut off, each with the likely break and its distance, and a reminder: needs field verification. |
| 1:40–2:00 | Tabs 'Rescue priorities' and 'Landing zones'; 'Later passes' (case study: possible lakes forming); then the situation report PDF and the field package. | Settlements are ranked for rescue, with helicopter landing-zone candidates nearby. Later satellite passes show whether a lake is forming behind debris. And it writes a one-page situation report and a field package for GPS units and phones. |
| 2:00–2:22 | Tab 'AI model': the model's water map and its held-out scores. | Our AI component is our own flood segmentation model, trained from scratch on Sen1Floods11. On held-out test images it clearly beats a simple threshold rule. In Himalayan gorges it transfers poorly, and we show that honestly: it is a second opinion, not the main map. |
| 2:22–2:45 | validation/results.md table and the Bidur comparison map (EMSR927_AOI03_GRA_PRODUCT_v1.png); then docs/testing/unseen_places.md. | We checked our results against the official Copernicus EMS maps, which were never an input. Where we flag damage, EMS agrees, with ninety-one to one hundred percent precision. In the narrow gorge we find only about a third of the area, a limitation we document. We also ran the system on 13 places it had never seen. |
| 2:45–2:55 | Tab 'Data & limitations'. | Satellites pass every few days, so this maps the damage. It cannot warn minutes before a glacier collapse. |
| 2:55–3:00 | End card (end_card.png), hold until the end. | Sampark is an educational prototype. Always verify on the ground. |
